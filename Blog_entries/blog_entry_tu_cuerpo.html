Organ Speak: What It Really Means to listen to Our Bodies

Giulia Enders · Greystone Books

This book changed the way I see myself. Not metaphorically: it literally changed how I understand what happens inside me when I breathe, when I eat, when I sleep, when I get frightened. Giulia Enders has a rare gift for explaining human biology from a very physical perspective.

These pages combine scientific rigour with humour, genuine wonder, and a perspective that includes human behaviour in a way that is anything but usual.

What it is really about

The original German title is Darm mit Charme — something like "The Charming Gut" — and throughout the book Enders connects the workings of the intestine with the nervous system, the immune system, mood, and yes, with breathing.

One of the chapters I find most valuable is the one on the vagus nerve: that two-way communication cable between the brain and the abdomen that explains why a sudden fright unsettles your stomach, or why breathing deeply calms you down. The vagus nerve is, to a great extent, the highway along which the breathing signal travels to the autonomic nervous system. Understanding how it works can change the way you approach any breathing technique.

There is also an illuminating chapter on sleep, another on the microbes that inhabit us, and several on how the digestive system processes not only food but also accumulated stress and emotions.

Why I recommend it before others

If you are beginning to explore the physiology of breathing and do not know where to start, this book is a good option. Not because it is the most specialised — it is not — but because it gives you the conceptual foundation that makes everything else fall into place.

After reading it, books like Neuroscience of the Body by Nazaret Castellanos or works on the autonomic nervous system land very differently. Enders lays the groundwork. And she does it well.

It is also, simply, a very enjoyable read. There are few popular science books that leave you finishing a chapter on digestion with a smile. This one manages it.
