Vitamins D3 and K2: a duo that works better together

Why I take them combined, what each one does, and why I still prefer sunlight as their best source.

Vitamin D is technically a hormone, not a vitamin. Its receptors are present in virtually every tissue in the body: muscle, bone, immune system, brain, pancreas. A deficiency — being below 40 ng/ml in the blood — is one of the best predictors of risk for many of the diseases we know, and it affects a surprisingly high proportion of the population even in sunny countries like Spain.

Sunlight, always first

Cutaneous synthesis of vitamin D through sun exposure is the natural and most effective route. If you live in Spain and can expose your arms, legs and/or back to the sun for 15–30 minutes a day in the first or last three hours of daylight, you probably do not need to supplement. Sunscreen, clothing, and light passing through car or house windows all block the frequencies of light that trigger synthesis.

Early morning and late afternoon sun baths have an additional chronobiological benefit — circadian rhythm regulation via red and infrared light — but sunlight outside those hours also has benefits that outweigh the drawbacks for almost all of the year. The exception: the months from April to September, when sun exposure during the middle of the day can be harmful to our cellular health — especially if our body is not accustomed to it, since its intensity can catch us without the natural protective mechanisms we develop through moderate and continuous exposure to direct solar radiation.

Why D3 and not D2

There are two supplemental forms: D2 (ergocalciferol, plant-derived) and D3 (cholecalciferol, animal-derived or from lichen). D3 is the same form the skin synthesises and has greater biological potency and a longer half-life. For supplementation, D3 is the standard choice.

Why add K2

Vitamin D increases the intestinal absorption of calcium. Vitamin K2 (menaquinone, especially the MK-7 form) activates the proteins that direct that calcium toward bones and teeth, rather than allowing it to deposit in the arteries. Taken together, they work synergistically: D3 absorbs the calcium, K2 places it where it belongs.

I take both with breakfast, always with fat (eggs, avocado, olive oil, butter…): they are fat-soluble vitamins and their absorption depends on the presence of fat in the intestine. Taking them on an empty stomach or with a very low-fat breakfast significantly reduces their bioavailability.

Combining them with Omega-3 provides additional anti-inflammatory synergies. That is why breakfast is the moment I concentrate these three micronutrients together.
