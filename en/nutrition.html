Module · Conscious nutrition

Because, for now, I don't live on air alone

My approach starts from a simple idea: food should be the main source of nutrients. In certain periods, a whole-food diet may not cover all needs. In those cases, some supplements can serve as temporary support.

This list is not a purchase or brand recommendation. It simply reflects the products I use occasionally. I always prioritise real food and adjust supplementation based on my dietary habits and sun exposure.

The water we drink

Water filter View on Amazon →

Before talking about supplements, I think it's worth talking about water. I live in Madrid, and tap water quality is quite good here — though it does contain chlorine and some suspended particles I'd rather avoid. In other areas, tap water may contain certain amounts of heavy metals, limescale and other residues that, while within legally safe levels, are not entirely neutral for the body or for taste over the long term.

After looking into reverse osmosis filtration systems, considering bottled water, and even thinking about a Kangen water generator, I went with this under-sink filter that works at the point of use. It removes up to 99% of common contaminants — chlorine, lead, nitrates, microplastics — and noticeably improves the taste. I use it daily for both drinking and cooking.

Unlike jug filters, it requires no refilling and has no significant flow limitations. Installation is straightforward and maintenance comes down to replacing the cartridge periodically as the manufacturer recommends. In my case, once a year with the RF10 model, or every two years with the RF15 model — you only need to replace the filter cartridges, which are available separately.

Supplements

Omega-3 (EPA/DHA) View on Amazon →

I use this when my intake of oily fish (sardines, salmon, mackerel) drops, or when I increase intake of seeds rich in other fatty acids. I look for formulations with significant EPA and DHA content, ideally with oxidation control and purity standards. On an empty stomach it can cause reflux if taken alone…

📝 Why I chose this omega-3
Vitamin C View on Amazon →

I use it as immune and antioxidant support, split across several daily doses. I find it especially useful combined with foods naturally rich in vitamin C (kiwi, citrus, red pepper, berries). One gram per day in two or three doses improves absorption and reduces the risk of kidney stones. Note: it affects blood test results.

📝 Why I chose this vitamin C
Psyllium View on Amazon →

A source of soluble fibre with prebiotic effect. I use it to reach recommended daily fibre levels. Always take with plenty of water and separate from other supplements to avoid interference with mineral and vitamin absorption.

Selenium View on Amazon →

A mineral involved in antioxidant and immune functions. Note: excess is often detected through brittle nails. I would not extend use beyond 15–30 days per quarter.

Zinc (Bisglycinate) View on Amazon →

A well-tolerated form with good bioavailability. I use it for immune support. Do not extend use beyond 15 days every 3 months without reviewing copper and iron levels.

Potassium (Citrate) View on Amazon →

Useful in diets high in sodium and low in fruit and vegetables. I prefer to prioritise obtaining it through fresh food and limiting sodium intake.

Vitamin D3 / K2 View on Amazon →

Especially useful when sun exposure is limited. Take with fat-containing food for better absorption. Combined with Omega-3, it has anti-inflammatory synergies. Best time: breakfast with fat.

Magnesium (Citrate) View on Amazon →

A mineral frequently insufficient in the diet. I use it mainly to support muscle relaxation and sleep quality. The citrate form is reasonably well absorbed and significantly more affordable than malate or bisglycinate.

Compatibilities and synergies

Do not take together
  • Zinc + Magnesium (compete)
  • Zinc + Iron / Calcium
  • Psyllium + any supplement (reduces absorption)
  • Magnesium + Psyllium (reduces bioavailability)
  • High-dose Vitamin C + Zinc (may irritate GI)
Useful synergies
  • Vitamin D + fat (avocado, olive oil, eggs)
  • Omega-3 + fatty meal (better absorption)
  • Magnesium + light dinner → improves sleep
  • Vitamin C + polyphenols (kiwi, pepper)
  • Zinc + animal protein (better absorption)
