Neurociencia del cuerpo (Neuroscience of the Body): the body is not the mind's support, it is part of it

Nazaret Castellanos · Editorial Kairós · Available in Spanish only

After reading this book, I looked at my breathing with different eyes. Not because it taught me a new technique, but because it gave me the conceptual framework that explains why breathing does what it does. Nazaret Castellanos is a neuroimaging researcher and science communicator, and in this book — her major bestseller — she achieves something few manage: making the latest neuroscience accessible without sacrificing rigour.

The central idea that changes everything

The book's main argument is deceptively simple: the body is not merely the vessel in which the mind lives. It is an active part of how we think, feel and make decisions. This may sound philosophical, but it is backed by decades of research in cognitive and embodied neuroscience.

Castellanos explores how posture, gesture, physical sensations and, above all, breathing shape our inner experience in ways the conscious mind neither controls nor even detects. The body processes, filters and colours reality before we have formed any thought about it.

For those of us who practise breathing techniques, this has direct implications: we are not simply "relaxing". We are intervening in the neural circuits that regulate attention, emotional memory, the stress response and the capacity to be present.

What I got most from it

The chapter on heart rate variability (HRV) and its relationship with breathing was a piece that clicked into place alongside others I already had. HRV — the variation in time between heartbeats — is one of the most robust indicators of autonomic nervous system health. And the most effective way to influence it is, precisely, slow and deep breathing. Castellanos explains this with a clarity I had not found in other texts.

There is also an excellent chapter on body memory: how the body stores emotional experiences that the conscious mind has "forgotten", and how certain postures, chronic muscular tensions or breathing patterns are, literally, the physical imprint of that history.

The book gives no recipes. It gives understanding. And understanding, in this field, is more valuable than any protocol.

Who I recommend it to

Anyone who wants to understand why breathing techniques, mindfulness or conscious movement work — from a neuroscience perspective.

It is a book you can read with genuine interest in the subject and no prior scientific background. Castellanos takes great care with language without sacrificing substance. That balance is hard to find, and she achieves it.
