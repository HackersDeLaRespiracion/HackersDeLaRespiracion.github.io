Why I chose this omega‑3 (and not another)

How I compare omega‑3 supplements, which criteria I use, and why I prioritize the real EPA and DHA content, their ratio, and cost per effective gram.

For years I've heard about the benefits of omega‑3 as an "anti‑inflammatory" and a "heart protector." After listening to Dr. Carlos Jaramillo (YouTube), I realized not all omega‑3 supplements are equal: what matters is the real amount of EPA and DHA and their ratio. And of course, value for money. In this post I explain why I chose the omega‑3 I link to.

What I look for in an omega‑3

When my intake of fatty fish drops (sardines, mackerel, salmon), I use a supplement, choosing it based on three criteria: effective active dose, balanced EPA/DHA ratio, and a price that makes sense long term. (YouTube)

Following recommendations from clinicians whose consistency I've followed for years (YouTube), I always review:

  • Daily EPA + DHA amount, aiming close to ~2 g in high anti‑inflammatory demand. (YouTube)
  • EPA to DHA ratio, ideally around 1.5:1 (e.g., ~600 mg EPA / 400 mg DHA).
  • Chemical form and oxidation control to improve absorption and avoid rancid products. (YouTube)

The product I use: dose, ratio and price

On my website, within the Conscious Nutrition section, I link to a fish‑based omega‑3 supplement that I take specifically on days when my diet does not reach what I consider optimal levels. I mainly use it when I reduce fatty fish intake or increase other fats, to maintain balance between pro‑inflammatory omega‑6 and anti‑inflammatory omega‑3.

My choice is based on high dose per capsule, an EPA/DHA ratio close to 3:2, and a competitive price per gram of EPA + DHA compared with similar high‑quality products.

An example of what I avoid: some "premium" fish oils highlight the number of softgels but, when you check the label, provide only 200 mg EPA and 100 mg DHA per dose — far below what is typically needed for meaningful impact.

Conclusions

My omega‑3 approach is essentially clinical: reduce systemic inflammation, support arterial health, improve brain function, and assist recovery during periods of high physiological demand.

The daily dose should be sufficient to modulate inflammatory mediators. The EPA/DHA ratio should respect the natural model (~1.5:1). I use it strategically: when my diet lacks fatty fish, or during periods of stress, inflammation, or higher cognitive demand.
